The Unit Circle is a circle of radius 1. Its circumference corresponds to all points of $(x, y)$ that satisfies the equation $x^2 + y^2 = 1$.

Any point $P$ along the unit circles' circumference, has the c\oordinates $(P_x , P_y) = (\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$.
$\cos\theta$ corresponds to the x-coordinate of the point $P$ 
$\sin\theta$ corresponds to the y-coordinate of the point $P$ 

The main values to remember in the unit circle are:
- $\frac{\pi}{6}$ / $30\deg$  : $(\cos\theta = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \sin\theta = \frac{1}{2})$
- $\frac{\pi}{4}$ / $45\deg$  : $(\cos\theta = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \sin\theta = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})$
- $\frac{\pi}{3}$ / $60\deg$  : $(\cos\theta = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \sin\theta = \frac{1}{2})$

When $\theta = 0, \theta = 90\deg / \frac{\pi}{2}$ , the value of point $P$ is (1, 0) and (0, 1) respectively.

![[Pasted image 20260923145238.png]]

By *remembering* the coordinates x and y of point $P$ in the first quadrant (top-right), you can easily identify the values of points all across the circumference of the unit circle.

First quadrant: Positive x-coordinate (cos), Positive y-coordinate (sin)
Second quadrant: Negative x-coordinate (cos), Positive y-coordinate (sin)
Third quadrant: Negative x-coordinate (cos), Negative y-coordinate (sin)
Fourth quadrant: Positive x-coordinate (cos), Negative y-coordinate (sin)

### Non-Unit Circle

![[Pasted image 20260923152935.png]]

For circles with radius $\neq$ 1, the coordinates of point $P$ are x: $r\cos\theta$ and y: $r\sin\theta$ .
